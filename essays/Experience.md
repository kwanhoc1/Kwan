## Experience
A human being becomes oneself from experiencing sets of semi-random events. Me taking ICS 414 this spring semester is one of the semi-random events that happened to my life. My life was not programmed to take this class from the day of my birth (Or was it?). From my experience in ICS 414, I didn't only learn software engineering but also other important life lessons.

## The Class
ICS 414 is a project based software engineering class. It was designed to give us the experience of working with real clients in the tech industry. We were assigned to a team and we had to work on a semester long project. In the first week of the semester, I remember that only three out of six teammates showed up to class. One of my teammates who showed up was worried and overwhelmed by the project. That teammate's attitude showed me that she's a person who is strongly responsible and is ready for the project. That was the first thing I learned from ICS 414 this semester. The attitude you carry builds how people perceive you.

In the project we were assigned to do this semester, we had to modify the past semester's project and have a client meeting every two weeks to make changes accordingly. Our instructor, Professor Moore, took the role as the client, and he gently guided us to better solutions to what we were trying to achieve and implement. It was good practice for what we might have to go through as software engineering professionals. We specifically had to design a website that keeps track of food inventory. We updated the website according to user feedback and client meetings, but we were given a lot of freedom to do what we wanted. We could be as creative as we wanted, and that freedom gave us good experiences of finding different ways to implement features and fixing problems that we ran into.

## Who I Am
Working through this project by navigating client feedback, coordinating with teammates, and solving problems I hadn't anticipated, quietly pushed me toward a question I had never seriously sat with before. I was surrounded by people who saw me in different ways. Each person on my team had a version of me in their mind that I would never fully see. It made me ask something fundamental. Who am I? What makes me "myself"? Do my past deeds define who I am? Will my future deeds define who I will be? Or is my current presence in reality the only definition of me? How about how other people perceive me? Do countless versions of me in other people's minds define who I am? And there is a version of me that I think of myself. Does my perception of my teammates define who they are? Does my teammates' perception of me define who I am? I don't have a complete answer. I'm not sure anyone does. But I thought that maybe the journey to finding myself is just like a software that gets feedbacks from users. A software is never truly finished. It gets updated, patched, and improved with every round of feedback it receives. Every experience, every relationship, and every challenge we face is a kind of feedback that shapes the next version of who we are. ICS 414 was one of those updates for me.
